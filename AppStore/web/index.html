Your Hister index, everywhere you look

Searchister is a native client for Hister, the self-hosted search engine for everything you have read. Search your whole index from Spotlight, save pages from any app, and keep a searchable copy on your device for when the connection is not there.

Download on the App Store What is Hister? →

Searchister needs a Hister server of your own. It hosts nothing, has no accounts, and has no index of its own to sell you — it is a window onto the one you already run.

What it does

Four things a browser bookmark cannot.

🔍 Find it in Spotlight

Your saved pages turn up alongside everything else on your Mac or iPhone — matched on what the page says, not just what it is called. Opening a result takes you straight to the page in your browser.

✈️ Works offline

A searchable copy of your index lives on the device: every title, address and label, and the opening of every document's text. A search on a plane still answers. Full text arrives as you open things.

📥 Save from anywhere

The share sheet takes web pages, PDFs, Word documents, Markdown and plain text. Pages are captured with their title and readable text rather than filed as a bare link. Uploads queue and retry, so sharing works with no signal.

🗣️ Ask Siri

“Search Hister for…” and “Save to Hister” are available to Siri and to the Shortcuts app, so your index can be a step in an automation rather than a place you visit.

How it works

Run Hister

On a machine you control — a home server, a NAS, a small VPS. Hister indexes the pages you visit and the files you point it at. It is an independent, AGPL-licensed project, and it is not ours.

Point Searchister at it

Your server address and access token go in once. They are kept in the iCloud Keychain, so entering them on one device sets up the rest.

Stop thinking about it

The app keeps its copy of the index up to date in the background and publishes it to Spotlight. From then on the thing you half-remember reading is where you would look for it anyway.

The whole query language

Hister's query syntax works as it does on the server — fields, phrases, exclusions, alternatives, wildcards and sorting. A reference lives inside the app, so you do not have to hold it in your head.

Offline, the cached copy handles terms, phrases, negation and the title:, domain:, url: and label: filters. Sorting, regular expressions and date filters need the server.

What it doesn't do

Searchister talks to exactly one server: yours. Everything below is a statement about the shipping app, not an aspiration.

  • No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting
  • No accounts, and nothing to sign up for
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No access to your files, contacts, location, photos or camera
  • Nothing is sent to us — there is nowhere for it to go
